The Standby Hunt

Students calculate what devices cost while they do nothing. One 45-minute science lesson on power, energy, money and CO₂.

SubjectScience
Duration45 min
Age15–18
SkillsNumeracy · Digital · Green

Objectives

By the end, students can

  • tell power (W) from energy (kWh) and convert between Wh and kWh O1
  • calculate energy with E = P × t for a day and for a year O1
  • turn energy into cost and CO₂ using a given price and emission factor O1O4
  • use an interactive simulation to test "what if" decisions O2O3
  • name one standby habit they will change O4

O1–O7 refer to the project objectives in the application.

Materials

What you need

  • One phone, tablet or laptop per pair. The student module runs in the browser without login.
  • Projector for the opening question and the wrap-up
  • Calculator (phone calculator is fine)
  • Optional: a plug-in power meter and one real device, for example a games console or a speaker
  • Optional: sticky notes or a Padlet wall for the pledges

Lesson structure

45 minutes, six phases

  1. 5 min0–5

    1. Warm-up and pre-check

    Ask: "Which device at home uses electricity right now, although nobody is using it?" Collect three or four answers. Students open the module and answer the five pre-check questions alone. They see only their score.

  2. 7 min5–12

    2. Input: watts, hours, kilowatt-hours

    Use the Learn step on the projector. Move the sliders and let the class predict the yearly cost before you show it. Fix two ideas on the board: W is how fast, kWh is how much. 1 kWh = 1000 Wh.

  3. 15 min12–27

    3. Missions: restore the power

    Pairs solve four calculation missions. Each correct answer flips one breaker in the fuse box. Hints are built in. Walk around and ask pairs to explain their units, not only their numbers.

  4. 8 min27–35

    4. Home audit

    Pairs tick the devices they would switch off completely and read the yearly saving in kWh, euros and kg CO₂. If you have a power meter, measure one real device and let students replace the example value.

  5. 6 min35–41

    5. Post-check

    Five new questions of the same type, answered alone. The module shows explanations and compares pre and post score.

  6. 4 min41–45

    6. Reflection and pledge

    "What surprised you?" Each student writes one pledge, for example "I switch the console off at the socket." Post the pledges on the board or on Padlet.

Assessment

How you see progress

  • Pre and post score out of 5. One more correct answer equals 20 percentage points.
  • Number of missions solved, and whether hints were needed
  • Quality of the pledge: is it concrete and realistic?

Students tap "Copy my result" at the end and paste it where you collect results. The module stores nothing online and asks for no names.

Gamification tools

Built in and optional

  • Student module (this page): fuse-box missions, simulation, instant feedback
  • Mentimeter or Kahoot!: estimation poll in the warm-up, "How many euros per year does a console cost in standby?"
  • Padlet: pledge wall, shared with partner schools in TwinSpace

Inclusion

Differentiation

  • Language support: short sentences, units always written out once, pair work with a stronger English speaker
  • Maths support: every mission has a hint that gives the first step. Missions 1 and 2 need one formula only.
  • Fast pairs: change price and emission factor in the audit to your country's values and compare with a partner country.
  • Readability: high-contrast colours, a typeface designed for low vision, and full keyboard use.

Extension

Homework or next lesson

  • Find three devices at home with a standby light. Estimate their yearly cost.
  • Compare electricity prices in DE, PT, IS and TR. Why are they so different?
  • Design a one-page "Standby off" poster for the Erasmus corner.
